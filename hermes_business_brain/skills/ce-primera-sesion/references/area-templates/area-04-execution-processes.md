# Area 4 - Execution and processes

## Purpose

Capture how the work actually happens - the observed workflow with its triggers, decision points, exceptions and quality gates - so D04 records real execution, and X02/X04 capture the repeatable instructions and bounded projects worth keeping, instead of an idealized process.

## Open guided questions

1. Reconstruct a real job from confirmation to delivery. Where did the critical decisions happen?
2. What early sign tells you a delivery may go wrong?
3. Which part of your work follows a checklist, and which part needs your judgment?
4. What mistake has cost you the most money, rework or reputation, and what prevents it?
5. What does "done" or "accepted" mean for you in practice?
6. Who decides when something must be redone, and who pays for it?
7. What would a novice get wrong by following only your literal instructions?
8. What would stop your delivery if a tool, supplier or person disappeared tomorrow?
9. Which improvement did you make recently, and how did you know it worked?
10. What do you do when a customer asks for something outside the normal way?

## Signals to capture

- Real flow: trigger, inputs, outputs, owners - not the ideal version.
- Decision points, exceptions and escalation paths.
- Stopping signals and early warning signs.
- Quality evidence: what proves the work is finished and accepted.
- Dependencies on people, systems, materials and suppliers.
- Errors that are costly or hard to fix, and the control that prevents them.
- Lessons with an observed outcome versus speculation.

## Mapping table

| Matter heard | Suggested class | Entity type ids | When NOT to create a doc (keep in source) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observed delivery workflow, quality, capacity | D04 | proceso, servicio, herramienta | A one-off job never repeated: keep in source (or X04 as a case) |
| A repeatable process worth following as an instruction | X02 | proceso, protocolo | A process nobody can name the trigger for: keep in source until episodes exist |
| A bounded project, case or episode | X04 | proyecto, caso | Routine work without its own identity: keep within D04/source |
| A tool or system used to deliver | Entity record (critical resource records belong to area 7) | herramienta, implementacion | Using a tool proves nothing about ownership or contracts: note as entity, not a claim |

## External-AI prompt

> You are interviewing the owner of a small business about how the work actually gets done. Keep it a plain-language conversation, one open-ended question at a time, and refer to the person you are interviewing as "the owner". Ask them to walk through one real job from start to finish instead of describing an ideal process. No jargon (avoid words like "workflow", "SOP" or "deliverable"); let them name things as they do. Cover: the real steps of a recent job and where the key decisions were made; early signs that a job is going wrong; parts that follow a checklist and parts that depend on judgment; the most expensive mistake and what prevents it; what "done" or "accepted" really means; who decides about rework; what a novice would get wrong; what would stop delivery if a tool, supplier or person vanished; a recent improvement and how they knew it worked; and what happens when a customer asks for something unusual. Keep their story as told; do not turn their account into a procedure or a claim of fact.

## K-gap hints

- **K1:** the owner's definition of done/accepted; the judgment rule behind an exception.
- **K2:** a real example file, delivery log, incident record, or the tool's own history - recoverable execution evidence.
- **K3:** how a process would behave under a scenario never actually tried.
- **K4:** steps that never happen in practice and are not needed to understand delivery.
